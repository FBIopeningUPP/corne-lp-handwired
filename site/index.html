Thin, wireless, and yours to build

Most Corne builds are tall, tethered and need a PCB order. This one is none of those. It runs wireless with no cable between the halves, and it's slim enough that the two halves snap together back-to-back and slide into a bag.

Wireless split

A SuperMini nRF52840 in each half running ZMK, with a custom corne_lp keyboard definition and the stock Corne pinout.

Trackpad built in

A Cirque 23 mm GlidePoint on the right half, next to the thumb keys. Tap to click, or use the home-row mouse buttons.

Sculpted low-profile caps

CLP keycaps reworked for the KS-33 with a 2.1 mm longer stem, so you get the full 3.2 mm of travel. All 42 on one slicer plate.

Meet Blip

A 32 × 42 px robot drawn on each half's OLED by the firmware.

  • Typing: his hands swap on every key press.
  • Faster: sparks fly off his antenna, denser as your WPM climbs.
  • Over 70 WPM: a flickering flame crown appears.
  • Idle: he blinks and bobs.
  • Stop typing: he falls asleep, and the screens turn off after 30 s.

Keymap

Drawn to scale from the real layout. BASE in the centre of each key, NUM bottom-left, SYM bottom-right. Hold NUM + SYM for the ADJ layer (Bluetooth profiles, bootloader, reset).

Build one

About a weekend: a day of printing and an evening of soldering. Everything is open.

  1. Buy the partsAll off the shelf. The build guide has the full list with notes.
  2. Print the test kit25 minutes. Checks switch fit, magnet and insert holes, the reset button and a keycap.
  3. Print the case and capsShells, bases and all 42 keycaps. The keyboard needs no supports.
  4. Flash the firmwarePre-built .uf2 files for each half. Flash before you close anything.
  5. Wire it by handA printable wiring guide shows every half, pin and diode.
  6. AssembleBattery, SuperMini, OLED, switch, trackpad, then seven screws per half.
